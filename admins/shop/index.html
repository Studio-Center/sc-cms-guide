Shop

The Shop extension sells products through Stripe Checkout, PayPal, or both. Visitors pay on Stripe’s or PayPal’s own pages, so card details never touch your site. It suits a small catalog of products, tickets, or donations with fixed prices. Visitors can put several products in a basket, choose quantities, and pay for everything at once, or buy a single product straight away.

Setting up

Section titled “Setting up”
  1. Create a Stripe account, a PayPal business account, or both.
  2. Install and activate Shop under Extensions (see Extensions).
  3. In its Settings:
    • Payment providers: Stripe, PayPal, or Stripe and PayPal. With both, visitors see Pay by card and Pay with PayPal buttons. For PayPal’s settings, see PayPal below.
    • Stripe secret key: from the Stripe dashboard, Developers → API keys. Use a test key (sk_test_…) while trying it out. A restricted key works too: it needs write access to Checkout Sessions, read access to Events, and, for a fixed tax rate, write access to Tax Rates.
    • Currency: US dollar, Euro, Pound sterling, Canadian dollar, or Australian dollar.
    • Tax: No tax, Stripe Tax, or A fixed percentage.
      • Stripe Tax works out the right tax for each customer’s address. Turn it on in the Stripe dashboard first, and add where you’re registered to collect tax, or checkout fails.
      • A fixed percentage adds the same rate to every product (not to shipping). Set Tax rate (%), such as 20, and Tax name, such as VAT.
      • Tax only for orders shipping to: to charge a fixed percentage only in some states, as a sales tax, list their two-letter codes, such as VA. Leave it empty to tax every order.
        • Customers then choose Shipping to (one of those states, or Somewhere else) before they check out. The basket shows the tax as, for example, “VA Tax (6%), Virginia only”, and the total before it.
        • The shipping address the customer gives Stripe or PayPal is checked against their choice. With PayPal and API credentials, a different state is refused before anything is charged. With PayPal email only, the payment is listed under Orders → Needs review. With Stripe, the order is recorded with a Check line on its screen.
      • PayPal can’t use Stripe Tax: while Tax is Stripe Tax, PayPal isn’t offered. A fixed percentage works with both.
    • Prices include tax: on if your prices already include tax (usual for VAT); off to add tax on top at checkout.
    • Shipping per item: charged for each item bought, such as 2.50 (two mugs cost twice this). Leave it empty or 0 for free shipping.
    • Countries you ship to: two-letter country codes, such as US, CA, GB. Customers enter a shipping address in one of these when their order has anything that ships. It’s required if shipping costs anything.
    • Shipping name: what customers see at checkout, such as Standard delivery.
    • Message after paying: shown on the thank-you page.
  4. For Stripe, add a webhook pointing to https://<your site>/api/v1/ext/shop/webhook with the events checkout.session.completed and checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded. Without it, card payments still record, but slower methods such as bank debits won’t.

Upgrading from Shop 1.0

The update asks you to approve a new permission, cookies, which the basket needs. Your settings, Stripe key, products, and orders are kept.

PayPal

Section titled “PayPal”

Choose How PayPal is connected:

  • With API credentials (recommended). In PayPal Developer, open Apps & Credentials, create an app, and copy its Client ID and Secret into PayPal client ID and PayPal client secret. Then add a webhook to the app pointing to https://<your site>/api/v1/ext/shop/paypal/webhook, with the events Checkout order approved and Payment capture completed.
  • With just my PayPal email address. Enter the email of your PayPal business account in PayPal email address. Payments are recorded when PayPal’s payment notification arrives at https://<your site>/api/v1/ext/shop/paypal/ipn. If paid orders never appear, turn on Instant payment notifications in your PayPal account’s notification settings.

Set PayPal environment to Sandbox to try it with PayPal’s test accounts (from PayPal Developer → Sandbox accounts) before switching to Live. Sandbox credentials only work with Sandbox.

Which to choose:

API credentials Just an email
Setup A PayPal developer app (client ID and secret) and a webhook Only your PayPal email
When the payment is checked Before anything is charged: the shop confirms the amount and the shipping country with PayPal, then takes the payment After the payment: PayPal tells the shop, and the shop checks it matches the order
Wrong country or a changed amount Refused; nothing is charged The money arrives, but the payment is listed under Orders → Needs review for you to check and refund in PayPal
Thank-you page Shows the order straight away Says PayPal will email a receipt; the order appears once PayPal confirms it
PayPal’s view Current integration Older integration PayPal still supports, but no longer recommends for new sites

The extension’s Health line points out setup problems, such as PayPal turned on without credentials, or PayPal with Stripe Tax.

Products

Section titled “Products”

Open Products from the Shop extension’s page. It lists your products by name, with their code, SKU, price, number of pictures, options, and whether they’re featured and active. Click Add product to add one, or Edit on a row to change one. Everything about a product is in one place, and wherever it’s shown (its product page, product lists, Buy buttons) shows the change at once.

A product has:

  • Product name and Code. The code is how Buy buttons find the product: lowercase letters, digits, and hyphens, such as coffee-mug. It’s set when you add the product and can’t be changed later.
  • SKU (optional), your own stock number.
  • Price (from 0.50).
  • Short description, shown on its product page and in product lists. Leave a blank line between paragraphs.
  • Pictures: click Add pictures to open the media library. Click the pictures you want to tick them, in the order you want them, then Add. You can also search, open folders, or Upload… new ones there. Each picture shows as a tile. The first is the Main picture, used in lists. The ← and → buttons change the order, Change swaps one picture for another, and ✕ removes it. The product page shows them all.
  • Shipping per item (optional): leave it empty to use the store’s amount, enter this product’s own amount, or 0 for things that don’t ship, such as downloads and tickets. An order with nothing that ships skips shipping and the address altogether.
  • Product page: the address of the product’s own page, such as /about/shop/products/coffee-mug. Product lists link to it.
  • Featured: product lists can show featured products first, or only those.
  • Active: turn it off to stop selling a product for now. It’s then left out of lists, and its Buy buttons say This product isn’t available.

A product keeps the currency that was set when it was saved. If you change the currency later, save each product again.

Pictures used by products don’t show under Used on in the media library. Deleting one there removes it from the product.

Product options

Section titled “Product options”

A product can come in options such as sizes or colors. Under Options, fill in one row per option: the option’s name, such as Size, and its choices separated by commas, such as Small, Medium, Large. To charge more or less for a choice, add the difference after it: Large +2.00, Extra-Large +3.50 (or -1.00 to charge less). Leave both boxes of a row empty to remove that option.

The Buy button then shows a list for each option, and visitors must choose before adding to the basket or buying. Each choice is its own line in the basket (“Studio Center Hoodie — Size: Large”), and PayPal, Stripe, and your Orders list show the same name. Prices always come from what you saved here.

Remove this product, at the bottom of a product’s screen, deletes it. Its Buy buttons then say This product isn’t available. To stop selling a product for a while, turn off Active instead.

Selling on a page

Section titled “Selling on a page”

Add a Buy button block to any page and choose the Product in its settings. The block shows the product’s name, description, price, a quantity field, and two buttons:

  • Add to basket puts the product in the visitor’s basket at /shop/basket. There they can change quantities, remove items, and check out everything at once. The basket is remembered in their browser for 30 days.
  • Buy now skips the basket and pays for this product alone.

You can rename either button or turn one off in the block’s settings. Tick Only the choices and the basket button for a small version that fits under a product in a list of products: just the option lists (such as Size) and the basket button. Link to /shop/basket from your menu so visitors can find their basket.

Visitors who pay land on a thank-you page that lists what they bought. If they cancel, they go back to the basket, or to the page they came from after Buy now.

Adding to the basket

Section titled “Adding to the basket”

When a visitor clicks Add to basket, a box opens on the same page with what’s in their basket. There they can change quantities (press Enter or Update), remove an item, empty the basket, close the box and keep shopping, or check out. Visitors whose browser doesn’t run scripts go to the basket page instead.

What visitors call the basket (in the Shop’s settings) switches the wording to “Shopping Cart”, “Clear Cart”, and “Checkout”. Sites moved from Concrete use Cart.

Product pages

Section titled “Product pages”

Give each product a page, and put a Buy button on it with The product’s own page turned on. It shows:

  • the product’s name as the page’s main heading;
  • the price, the short description, the choices, and the basket button;
  • the product’s pictures beside them, the main one across and the others two to a row.

Clicking a picture opens it larger, with arrows to the others. Write the product’s longer details as the page’s own content, below the Buy button. Then enter the page’s address as the product’s Product page, so lists link to it.

Product lists

Section titled “Product lists”

The Product list block shows your active products, always as they are in Products. Adding a product, or changing its price or pictures, shows in every list straight away. In its settings:

  • Order: by name, newest first, by price, or featured first. Featured products only shows just those.
  • How many, and Page numbers to show that many a page with links to the others.
  • Products per row: 1 to 4. On phones there’s always one.
  • What each product shows: the main picture, the price, the short description, a Link to the product page button (with its label, such as “More Details”; leave it empty for none), and Add to basket with the product’s choices.

The name and picture link to the product’s page when it has one.

Updating to Shop 1.4

Section titled “Updating to Shop 1.4”

The update asks for a new permission, Add scripts to public pages, for the basket box. Until an administrator approves it under Extensions → Shop, the Buy buttons and basket are off.

Updating to Shop 2.0

Section titled “Updating to Shop 2.0”

The update asks for two new permissions: Show pictures from the media library (for product pictures) and Read published content (to find product pages by their address). Until an administrator approves them under Extensions → Shop, the Shop’s blocks and basket are off. Your products are kept as they were, active and without pictures. Add the pictures and product pages to each product afterwards. Sites moved from Concrete get them when the import is run again.

Orders

Section titled “Orders”

Orders lists paid orders: when, the provider (Stripe or PayPal), the items and quantities, subtotal, shipping, tax, total, the customer’s email, the shipping address, and a link to the payment in Stripe or PayPal. An order is recorded only after sc-cms has checked with Stripe or PayPal that the payment went through, and the customer’s basket is emptied then.

Needs review lists payments PayPal confirmed that don’t match an order: a different amount or currency, another PayPal account, or a country you don’t ship to. They aren’t counted as orders; check them in PayPal and refund them there if needed. Refunds, receipts, and customer emails are handled in Stripe and PayPal.

Every paid order starts as Awaiting processing. Open an order from Extensions → Shop → Orders to see its details and change its status to Processing, Shipped, Delivered, Will not deliver, or Returned. The links above the list show how many orders have each status and filter the list. Customers aren’t emailed when the status changes.
