Pages and posts

Everything you write lives under Content. Each kind of content is listed under Content in the sidebar: Pages and Posts are built in, and your site may have more, such as Projects or Team members (see Content types).

  • Pages are the lasting parts of the site: Home, About, Contact. Pages can sit under other pages: a page called Pricing under Services lives at /services/pricing.
  • Posts are dated articles. They live at /blog/<slug> and can be organized with Categories and Tags.

Finding content

Section titled “Finding content”

Editing a page you’re looking at. If you’re on a page of the site and want to change it, copy its address from the browser and paste it into Search (⌘K or Ctrl+K): the first result opens that page in the editor. Old addresses that redirect work too. To skip copying, add the Edit page button from Your account to your bookmarks bar, then click it on any page of the site.

My work (under Content, and on the dashboard) lists everything you’ve changed that isn’t published yet, the reviews waiting for you (or your own requests), and what you’ve scheduled.

The list for each type has:

  • a search box;
  • status filters: All, Drafts, Published, Unpublished changes, and Trash;
  • on sites with several languages, a language filter.

Each row shows the title, its status, its public address, and when it was last changed.

Status Meaning
Draft Never published. Visitors can’t see it.
Published Live, and the live version is the latest.
Changes not published Live, but there’s a newer saved draft that visitors don’t see yet.
Trashed Deleted, and restorable from the Trash.

Long lists have page numbers above and below the list. “Showing 26–50 of 928” tells you where you are, and the Per page menu (25, 50, or 100) is remembered for each list. The media library, People, the activity log, and Redirects work the same way.

Links from your old site that point to a later page of a list (for example ?pg=2) open that page of the list. Addresses typed in capitals (/About), with a slash at the end (/about/), or starting with /index.php take visitors to the page.

Finding and changing many items at once

Section titled “Finding and changing many items at once”

Above the list are the status filters (All, Drafts, Published, Unpublished changes, Trash) and one bar with the search box and, when your site has them, a language menu and a menu for each choice field (for example Gender or Union for talent). A choice applies as soon as you pick it; press Enter to search. Clear removes the search and filters. Filters stay while you switch status or change pages.

Each row has View ↗ (the live page, once published) and History.

To change several items, tick them (the box in the heading ticks every item on the page). A bar appears at the bottom of the window with how many are selected. Pick an Action and choose Continue…: Publish (warnings don’t stop it; problems that must be fixed do), Unpublish (take offline), Move to trash, or Set a field…. You see the items first and confirm with one button. Afterwards a summary says what changed and which items were skipped and why. Setting a field makes a new draft of each item, so publish them afterwards (you can do that in bulk too). Each bulk action is recorded in the activity log.

Creating content

Section titled “Creating content”

Choose New page (or New post) at the top right, type a title, and choose Create page (or Create post). The new item opens in the editor straight away. Nothing is public until you publish it.

If your site has AI assistance, Draft with AI (beside New page) writes a first draft from a short description.

The dashboard has shortcuts too (New page, New post, and Add media), and so does Search (⌘K or Ctrl+K).

Page settings

Section titled “Page settings”

With no block selected, the right-hand panel of the editor shows the item’s settings. Under the Title they’re grouped in sections you can open and close: Address, Details (the type’s fields), Categories and tags, Menus, Visibility, and Developer. The editor remembers which sections you keep open on this computer, and a section with something to fix opens by itself and says needs attention.

  • Title.

  • URL slug (Address): the last part of the address. It follows the title until you edit it yourself. Changing the slug of a published item is safe: the old address redirects to the new one.

  • Parent page (Address, pages only), or None (top level). Type part of a title and pick the page from the list; Change picks another, and × puts the page back at the top level. Pages that link to other items, such as Related posts, work the same way: type to find an item, and × removes it.

  • Fields for the type (Details), such as Summary, Excerpt, Featured image, or Related posts. Fields marked (required to publish) must be filled in before publishing.

  • Categories and tags (posts): tick existing ones, or type a new one in Add new… and press Enter.

  • Search title, Search description, and Sharing image. See Search and sharing.

  • Visibility:

    • Members only. See Memberships.
    • Hide from menus and automatic navigation: left out of automatic menus, page navigation, and breadcrumbs. Links you added to a menu yourself still show.
    • Hide subpages from automatic navigation (pages): its subpages are left out of automatic menus and page navigation.
    • Position in navigation (pages): lower numbers come first in automatic menus. Pages without a number follow, in alphabetical order.
    • Hide from page lists: left out of the blog, categories, month archives, and other lists.
    • Hide from site search: visitors searching your site won’t find it.
    • Hide from search engines: asks search engines not to show it and leaves it out of the sitemap.

    The page can still be opened from its address in every case. Like everything else on the page, these settings take effect when you publish.

  • Structured data (JSON-LD) (developers only): schema.org data for search engines, as JSON. It’s checked when you save.

  • Revision note (optional): a word about what changed, shown in the history.

Fields with several choices show a checkbox for each option; tick as many as apply.

Formatted text fields work like a small word processor: select words and use the toolbar above the box for bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, size, font, color, links (which can open in a new tab), and Clear formatting, or Ctrl+B, Ctrl+I, Ctrl+U, and Ctrl+K. Enter starts a new paragraph and Shift+Enter a new line. Pasted text keeps its words but not its styling.

Deleting and restoring

Section titled “Deleting and restoring”

Move to trash unpublishes the item and moves it to the Trash. To get it back, open Content → Trash (every type in one list, newest first) or the Trash filter of its list, and choose Restore. It comes back as a draft, so it isn’t public again until you publish it. Nothing in the trash is ever deleted for good by the editor.
